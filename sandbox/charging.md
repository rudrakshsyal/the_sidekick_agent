# US Public EV Charging Landscape

## Public charging points
The US public EV charging network has grown rapidly and now totals roughly **200,000 public charging points** nationwide, spread across about **70,000 stations**. That is enough to support a broad sales fleet footprint, but coverage and reliability still vary by region, charger type, and operator. For fleet planning, the main takeaway is that public charging is no longer niche; it is becoming a practical fallback and supplement to depot charging, especially in metro areas and along major corridors.

## Two major networks a fleet could rely on
**ChargePoint** is one of the largest and most established US charging networks, with a very large Level 2 footprint and a substantial DC fast-charging presence. It is widely available at workplaces, retail sites, and public locations, which makes it useful for mixed-use fleet charging and driver convenience.

**EVgo** is another major US network focused on fast charging. It has a strong public DC fast-charging footprint across many states, making it especially relevant for fleets that need quick turnarounds and route-based charging rather than long dwell times.

## Fleet takeaway
For a sales fleet, public charging is best treated as a resilience layer rather than the primary energy source. A reliable fleet strategy usually combines depot charging with access to a major public network such as ChargePoint or EVgo for overflow, regional trips, and on-the-road charging.
